Research

Research

My work spans three connected layers: the materials that absorb and convert energy, the models that make sense of visual and sensor data, and the systems that carry both into the field.

01 Research Statement

My core research asks how computational tools, device simulation, and machine learning can accelerate the design of sustainable technology under real-world constraints. In photovoltaics, this means screening lead-free perovskite absorbers computationally before they are ever grown in a lab, then validating device performance with SCAPS-1D and machine learning surrogates. In applied AI, it means building vision models that do not just perform well on curated benchmarks, but hold up when deployed on a real field, farm, or clinic, where lighting, background, and data quality are never ideal. I work on both because the underlying discipline is the same: build a model of a system, stress-test it against reality, and only then trust its predictions.

02 Research Areas

Computational Materials & Photovoltaics

Device-level (SCAPS-1D) and DFT simulation of lead-free perovskite solar cells, with machine learning used to validate and explain device performance trends.

CsBi₃I₁₀ heterojunction cells CsGeI₃ absorbers Mg₃AsX₃ strain effects Gradient Boosting + SHAP

Robust Machine Learning for Real-World Vision

Object detection and Vision Transformer models for agricultural and veterinary applications, built to survive the gap between lab-quality training data and messy field conditions.

YOLOv8 — livestock disease screening DeiT-Small ViT — rice disease diagnosis Attention-consistency regularization Domain shift / faithfulness-aware explanation

Embedded Systems & IoT

Two-tier sensing and cloud-integrated monitoring systems for energy and environmental applications, built on constrained, low-cost hardware.

Arduino / ESP32 Real-time water quality monitoring Biogas & off-grid solar monitoring Firebase cloud integration
